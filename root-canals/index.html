Root Canal Treatment in Miami & Hialeah

A root canal removes the infected or inflamed pulp inside a tooth so you can keep the tooth instead of losing it. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we do root canal treatment for patients from all over Miami, using a dental microscope to see inside the tooth.

What is a root canal?

Inside every tooth is the pulp, soft tissue with nerves and blood vessels. When decay or a crack lets bacteria reach it, the pulp gets inflamed or infected, and that infection won't heal on its own. A root canal cleans out the pulp, disinfects the canals and seals them, and a crown then protects the tooth.

  1. 1Infected pulp Inflamed tissue inside the tooth
  2. 2Abscess Infection that spread to the root tip
  3. 3Filled canals Cleaned, disinfected and sealed
  4. 4Crown Protects the treated tooth

Do you need a root canal?

Only an exam and an X-ray can tell for sure, but these signs are worth getting checked soon.

Signs to get checked

  • Pain when you chew or touch the tooth
  • Pain that spreads to your jaw, face or nearby teeth
  • A pimple or boil on the gum that may ooze pus
  • Tender or swollen gums, or a swollen jaw
  • A tooth that has turned darker or feels loose

When saving the tooth may not work

  • A crack that runs deep into the root
  • Too little healthy tooth left to rebuild
  • Severe bone loss around the tooth

If a root canal isn't likely to work, we'll tell you and go over taking the tooth out and replacing it.

Treatment under the microscope

We use a dental microscope to see inside the tooth in detail while we clean the canals, and rotary instruments to shape them.

The area is numbed first, so for most people the treatment feels no worse than getting a filling.

You keep your tooth

Saving the natural tooth means no gap to fill with an implant or a bridge.

Often one visit

Most root canals take about 60 to 90 minutes, and many are done in one sitting.

Built to last

With a crown and good care, a treated tooth can last for many years, often a lifetime.

How a root canal is done, step by step

  1. 1

    Exam and X-ray

    We find the source of the pain and check how far the infection has gone.

  2. 2

    Numbing

    We numb the tooth and the area around it. You stay awake and comfortable.

  3. 3

    Cleaning the canals

    Through a small opening in the top of the tooth, we remove the infected pulp, then clean and shape the canals.

  4. 4

    Sealing

    We fill the canals with a rubbery material and close the opening with a temporary filling.

  5. 5

    Crown

    A permanent crown goes on later to protect the tooth and restore your bite. That usually takes two more appointments.

Recovery after a root canal

Most people recover in less than a week.

Day of treatment
Wait until the numbness wears off before eating, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue.
First few days
Mild soreness is normal. Over-the-counter pain relievers usually handle it. Stick to soft foods like yogurt, smoothies or pasta.
Until the crown
Don't bite down hard on the treated tooth until the permanent crown is in place.
After that
Brush, floss and keep your checkups like with any other tooth.

Call us if the swelling comes back, the pain gets worse after a few days, or the temporary filling comes out.

What can go wrong

Most root canals work. These are the cases where they don't, and what we do then.

  • If the damage is too severe, a root canal may not work.
  • A treated tooth can get infected again, and then it may need a second root canal.
  • Without a crown, a treated back tooth is more likely to crack.
  • Some soreness for a few days afterward is normal.

When a root canal can't save the tooth, the options are re-treatment or taking it out and replacing it with an implant or a bridge.

How much does a root canal cost in Miami?

The price depends on the tooth and what it needs, so we give you a cost estimate after the exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • Which tooth it is. Molars have more canals than front teeth
  • Whether it's a first treatment or a re-treatment
  • The crown that protects the tooth afterward
  • What your insurance covers

We work with many dental plans, including Delta Dental, MetLife, Cigna, Aetna and Humana, and we check your coverage before you start. Many plans pay part of a root canal. Root canal questions

How long does a root canal take?

Most root canals take about 60 to 90 minutes. Many are done in one visit, but an infected or complex tooth may need two. The crown is placed afterward, usually over two more appointments.

Does a root canal hurt?

The tooth is numbed, so for most people it feels no worse than getting a filling. Mild soreness for a few days afterward is normal, and over-the-counter pain relievers usually handle it.

How do I know if I need a root canal?

Pain when you chew, pain that spreads to your jaw, a pimple on the gum, swelling or a tooth that turns darker are common signs. Only an exam and an X-ray can confirm it, so call us if you notice any of them.

Can a tooth heal without a root canal?

No. Once the pulp inside a tooth is infected, the infection won't heal on its own. Antibiotics can calm swelling for a while, but the tooth needs a root canal or an extraction.

Can I eat and drive after a root canal?

With local anesthesia only, you can usually drive yourself home. Wait until the numbness wears off before eating, choose soft foods for a few days and don't bite hard on the tooth until the crown is on.

Are root canals bad for your health?

No. The American Association of Endodontists says there's no valid scientific evidence linking root canal treatment to other diseases, including cancer. Leaving an infected tooth untreated is what causes harm.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

Usually, yes, especially on back teeth. The crown protects the treated tooth from cracking and restores your bite.
