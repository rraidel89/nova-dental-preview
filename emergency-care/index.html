What counts as a dental emergency?

A dental emergency is anything that needs a dentist soon to stop pain, save a tooth or keep an infection from spreading. That includes a severe toothache, a cracked or broken tooth, a knocked-out tooth, an abscess and injuries to the gums or lips. Something stuck between your teeth usually isn't one.

Call us or go to the ER?

Most dental emergencies are treated at the dental office. A few need the emergency room first.

Call us today

  • A severe toothache
  • A cracked, chipped or broken tooth
  • A knocked-out tooth
  • A lost filling, crown or bridge
  • Swelling or a pimple on the gum around a tooth

Go to the ER or call 911

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing
  • Swelling in your face or neck that's spreading, especially with a fever
  • Bleeding that won't stop with pressure
  • A possible broken jaw or other facial injury

Our office is open Monday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. If we're closed and the pain is severe, go to the nearest emergency room.

What to do before you get here

These first steps can make the difference between saving a tooth and losing it.

Knocked-out tooth
Pick it up by the crown, not the root. Rinse it briefly if dirty and try to put it back in the socket. If you can't, keep it in milk. Come in right away. The sooner the better, ideally within 30 minutes.
Broken tooth
Save the pieces, rinse your mouth with warm water and hold a cold compress on your cheek.
Severe toothache
Rinse with warm water and floss out any trapped food. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever. Never put aspirin on the gum, because it can burn it.
Lost filling or crown
Keep the crown and bring it with you. Sugarless gum or dental cement from the pharmacy can cover the hole for now.
Swelling or abscess
Rinse gently with mild salt water and call us. An abscess can damage the tissue and the teeth around it.

If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, or your face is swelling fast, call 911 or go to the emergency room.

Why waiting makes it worse

Dental pain sometimes fades, but the problem behind it usually doesn't.

  • An infected tooth won't heal on its own, and the infection can spread to the jaw and face.
  • A crack can grow deeper until the tooth can't be saved.
  • A knocked-out tooth has the best chance when it's back in its socket within the first hour.
  • An abscess can damage the bone and the teeth around it.

If you're not sure whether it can wait, call us and we'll tell you.

How much does an emergency visit cost?

The visit starts with an exam and X-rays. The rest depends on what the tooth needs, and we tell you the cost before we treat. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • The exam and X-rays
  • The treatment needed: a filling, a root canal, a crown or an extraction
  • Whether you need a temporary fix first and a permanent one later
  • What your insurance covers

We work with many dental plans, including Delta Dental, MetLife, Cigna, Aetna and Humana, and we check your coverage before you start. Most plans cover part of an emergency exam and X-rays. Original Medicare doesn't cover most dental care, though some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits. No insurance? Ask about our dental membership plan and flexible payment plans.

Dental emergency questions

What should I do if a tooth gets knocked out?

Pick it up by the crown, rinse it briefly if it's dirty and try to put it back in the socket. If it won't go back, keep it in milk and come in right away. A tooth has the best chance when it's back in place within an hour, ideally within 30 minutes. Baby teeth aren't put back.

Is a broken filling a dental emergency?

It's urgent but rarely an emergency. Call us for an appointment soon. Until then, sugarless gum or temporary dental cement can cover the hole, and avoid chewing on that side.

When should I go to the ER for tooth pain?

Go to the ER if you have trouble breathing or swallowing, your face or neck is swelling, you have a fever with tooth pain or bleeding won't stop. For a toothache without those signs, call your dentist first.

Can I see a dentist the same day?

We offer same-day appointments for urgent cases when there's room in the schedule. Call as early as you can and tell us what happened, so we can prioritize your visit.

What can I take for a toothache?

Over-the-counter pain relievers such as acetaminophen, ibuprofen or naproxen can help while you wait, following the label. Rinse with warm water and don't put aspirin on the gum, because it can burn the tissue.

Do you see emergencies on weekends?

We're open on Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. We're closed on Sundays, so if the pain is severe then, go to the nearest emergency room.
