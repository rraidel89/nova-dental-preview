Children's Dentistry in Miami & Hialeah

Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah welcomes children age five and older for examinations, cleanings, preventive care, fillings and extractions according to their needs. We explain each step in a friendly way and guide families on home care. We also evaluate growth and bite development and offer early interceptive orthodontics when indicated.

Why do baby teeth matter?

Baby teeth help your child chew and speak, and they guide the permanent teeth into position. As a permanent tooth forms below, the baby tooth's root gradually dissolves until the tooth falls out. Children lose them over several years, roughly from age 6 to age 12. A cavity left untreated can cause pain, infection and swelling, and it can damage the adult tooth developing underneath.

  1. 1Baby molar Helps your child chew and holds space for the next tooth
  2. 2Root being absorbed Dissolves little by little before the tooth falls out
  3. 3Permanent tooth Develops in the jaw under the baby tooth
  4. 4Young permanent molar Usually comes in around age 6 to 7

Is our office a good fit for your child?

We see children age five and older for general dental care. Some situations call for another kind of care, and we'll tell you if that's the case.

We can help when

  • Your child is five or older and due for a checkup and cleaning
  • You see a dark spot or hole in a tooth, or your child complains of a sore tooth
  • A baby tooth may need a filling or an extraction
  • You're concerned about crowded teeth, your child's bite or a thumb-sucking habit
  • You'd like help with brushing, flossing and snacks at home

Call first or look for other care

  • Your child is younger than five. We don't see children under five, and the CDC advises a first dental visit by the first birthday
  • Your child has strong fear of treatment or special care needs. Call us so we can talk through whether we're the right place
  • Treatment would require sedation. We don't offer it as a routine part of children's care
  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding that won't stop or severe swelling spreading through the face or neck needs emergency care right away

Call 786-687-0909 to request a visit for your child. We're open Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Saturday by appointment. Our front desk checks availability and confirms your appointment.

How we care for children at Nova

We explain each step in words your child can follow and give you practical guidance for home. Our general dentists see children for routine and restorative care based on what each child needs.

We also keep an eye on growth and how the teeth come together. An evaluation doesn't mean your child needs immediate treatment. If early orthodontics may help, our orthodontics page covers expanders, crossbites and habits.

Problems found sooner

The CDC says more than half of children aged 6 to 8 have had a cavity in a baby tooth. Decay can worsen with time, so a checkup can help catch it sooner.

Habits that fit home

We show you and your child how to brush and floss, and we talk about diet, fluoride and habits like thumb-sucking.

Bite checked while growing

Around age seven, many children have a mix of baby and permanent teeth, which helps show how the bite and jaws are developing.

Checkups and prevention

A regular visit includes an exam, a cleaning when indicated and advice for home care.

  • Exam of the teeth, gums and bite
  • Cleaning of plaque and stains
  • X-rays when your child's case calls for them
  • Fluoride or dental sealants, if we recommend them for your child

Fillings and extractions

Treatment depends on how far the decay has gone and how the tooth is doing. We explain the options to you before we start.

  • Fillings to repair small cavities
  • Extractions when decay has reached the nerve and causes pain or infection
  • Local anesthesia when the procedure requires it
  • Care instructions to take home

Growth and bite

We look at how the teeth and jaws are developing and tell you what we see.

  • Crowding, crossbites and teeth that don't meet
  • Early or late loss of baby teeth
  • Thumb or finger habits that affect the teeth
  • Early interceptive orthodontics when indicated

After a filling or extraction: what to expect

Numbness usually wears off within a few hours. Keep an eye on your child during that time and follow the instructions we give you.

While numb
Numbness often lasts 2 to 4 hours. Remind your child not to bite, suck or touch the lip, cheek or tongue. A bitten lip can become a sore ulcer that hurts for 3 to 4 days.
Eating
Offer soft, cool food and nothing hot until the feeling returns. After an extraction, have your child chew on the other side.
The day of an extraction
Keep your child resting or playing quietly, with no exercise. No spitting or rinsing for 24 hours. Brush as usual at bedtime and skip the treated area.
If the area bleeds
Have your child bite firmly on a damp piece of gauze for at least 10 minutes. Repeat once with fresh gauze if needed.
The next few days
Mild soreness and swelling are normal. Use a children's pain reliever such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen, following the package directions.
After a filling
Fillings can fall out or get damaged. Call us if you notice one has come loose or your child feels a change in the tooth.

Trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding that won't stop or severe swelling spreading through the face or neck needs emergency care: call 911. Call us at 786-687-0909 if bleeding continues after two rounds of gauze, swelling or discomfort isn't improving, or a lip sore hasn't healed after 7 days.

Filling or extraction of a baby tooth?

When a cavity can be repaired, a filling lets your child keep the tooth. How far the decay has gone and the tooth's condition guide the decision.

When it's used A cavity that can still be repaired Decay that reached the nerve and causes pain or infection
What happens We remove the decay and fill the space We numb the area and take the tooth out

Is dental treatment safe for children? Risks to know

Every treatment carries some risk, and we choose care based on your child's health and needs. These are the things parents should know about.

  • Biting the lip, cheek or tongue while numb. This can leave a painful sore, so watch your child until the numbness is gone
  • Pain, bleeding, bruising, swelling or infection after an extraction
  • A filling can fall out or get damaged, and a sealant can wear or chip and may need replacing
  • Early loss of a baby molar can let nearby teeth drift. A small 2019 study found permanent premolars were more often out of line when the baby molar came out before age 8
  • X-rays use a small amount of radiation, so we take them only when they're indicated and at the lowest practical exposure

Tell us about any allergies, health conditions and medications your child takes. Some medicines may need to be paused around an extraction, and we'd go over that with you first.

Questions parents ask about children's dentistry

What age do you start seeing children?

We see children age five and older.

My child is younger than five. When should the first dental visit be?

The CDC advises a first dental visit by the first birthday. We don't see children under five, so ask your pediatrician to recommend a dentist who treats younger children. We'd be glad to see your child from age five.

Who will see my child?

Children are seen by our general dentists and team. If your child needs care we don't provide, we'll tell you and explain your options.

How often should my child see the dentist?

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends a visit every six months, or more or less often depending on your child's needs and risk of cavities.

What happens at a first visit?

We review your child's health, explain what we'll do, examine the teeth, gums and bite, and clean the teeth if indicated. We then go over what we found and how to care for the teeth at home.

Why fix a baby tooth if it's going to fall out anyway?

Some baby teeth stay until age 10 to 12. Decay can cause pain, infection and swelling, and it can damage the adult tooth developing below. Keeping the tooth also helps hold space for the permanent tooth.

Does a baby tooth with a large cavity have to come out?

Not always. Depending on the tooth, options can include a stainless steel crown or an extraction. We'll explain which apply to your child, and if we don't provide one of them, we'll tell you.

How can I prepare my child, and can I stay with them?

Choose a time when your child is rested, give a light meal and brush beforehand, and talk about the visit in a calm, positive way. You're welcome to stay with your child, and bring your questions.

Does my child need dental X-rays?

It depends on the child. Cleveland Clinic notes that most children have X-rays around age 5 or 6, when they can show decay, bite problems and whether adult teeth are developing. We take them only when indicated.

Does my child need sealants or fluoride?

We may recommend them based on your child's risk of cavities. Sealants are thin coatings on the chewing surfaces of back teeth. The CDC reports they prevent about 81% of cavities in permanent molars at two years and 50% at four years.

When should my child's bite be checked?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends an orthodontic evaluation no later than age seven, or sooner if you notice a problem. Treatment isn't necessary for every child, and we can tell you what we find.

Do you use sedation for children?

Sedation isn't a routine part of children's care with us. We explain each step and use local anesthesia when the procedure requires it. If your child needs something different, we'll talk with you about your options.
