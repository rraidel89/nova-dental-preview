Dental Fillings in Miami & Hialeah

A cavity can grow even before it hurts. As it progresses, it can cause sensitivity, pain, infection and loss of tooth structure, and may need more involved treatment. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we assess the remaining healthy tooth structure and whether a filling or another restoration can protect it. Request an evaluation to explore your options.

What is a dental filling?

A cavity is a hole in a tooth, caused by acids from plaque bacteria that break down the enamel. A filling repairs it: the decayed tissue is removed and the space is filled with a sturdy material. Fillings can also fix minor chips or cracks. Tooth-colored fillings use resin composite, a resin base mixed with powdered quartz, silica or glass.

  1. 1Cavity Decay that has made a hole in the tooth
  2. 2Pulp Nerves and blood vessels inside the tooth
  3. 3Tooth-colored filling Fills the space once the decay is removed

Do you need a filling?

You might not feel a cavity until it reaches the deeper layers of the tooth, and X-rays can show cavities you can't see. An exam is the way to know. These signs are worth getting checked.

Signs to get checked

  • Tooth pain or an achy feeling, especially after sweet, hot or cold foods and drinks
  • Sensitivity to hot and cold
  • Pits, holes or dark spots on a tooth
  • A minor chip or crack
  • An older filling that has broken
  • Bad breath or a bad taste that won't go away

When a filling may not be the right fix

  • A chalky white spot with no hole yet. Early decay may respond to fluoride treatments
  • Too much damage for a filling. An inlay, onlay or crown may fit better
  • Decay that has reached the pulp, which calls for a root canal
  • A tooth that can't be saved, which may need to come out and be replaced

We check how much healthy tooth remains and explain which option fits before you decide. Tell us about any allergic reaction you've had to dental materials.

How we choose your restoration

The amount of healthy tooth left guides the choice. A small to mid-size cavity can often take a filling, and a larger one may need an inlay, onlay or crown. We show you what we find and go over the options with you.

If dental visits make you nervous, tell us. We numb the area with local anesthesia, and nitrous oxide is the sedation option we offer if it suits your health.

Blends with your tooth

Composite is tooth-colored and can be used on front or back teeth.

Usually one visit

A composite filling is usually finished in one appointment, often in an hour or less.

Treats the decay

Removing the decayed tissue helps stop that cavity from growing and can help you avoid more involved treatment.

Tooth-colored filling

Resin composite is placed directly in the tooth and hardened with a curing light. According to the ADA, it offers good durability in small to mid-size fillings that handle moderate chewing pressure.

Inlays and onlays

These are custom-made pieces for a tooth with too much damage for a filling and enough healthy structure to avoid a crown. They usually take two visits.

Crowns

A crown covers most or all of the visible tooth. It's used when decay is extensive and little healthy structure remains, or to protect a weak or cracked tooth.

How a cavity is filled, step by step

  1. 1

    Exam and X-rays

    We check the tooth for soft spots and may take X-rays. Then we explain what we found and your options.

  2. 2

    Numbing

    We numb the tooth with local anesthesia. Tell us if you feel any discomfort during treatment.

  3. 3

    Removing the decay

    We remove the decayed or damaged tissue. Leaving it behind could let the cavity keep growing.

  4. 4

    Placing the filling

    We keep the tooth clean and dry, place the composite and harden it with a curing light.

  5. 5

    Polish and bite check

    We smooth any rough edges and check that your bite feels normal.

What to expect after a filling

Most people go back to their routine right after the visit. If you had nitrous oxide, we check that you're fully alert before you leave.

While you're numb
The numbness can last 2 to 7 hours. You can eat and drink without damaging the filling, but waiting until it wears off helps you avoid biting your cheek.
First days
Some soreness is normal. Over-the-counter pain relievers such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen can help.
First week or two
Mild sensitivity and gum soreness are normal and should fade within a week or two.
After that
Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, floss daily and keep your regular checkups.

Call us right away if the filling breaks, or if you have severe pain that medication doesn't relieve, a swollen face or jaw, fever or chills.

Filling or crown?

The choice depends on how much healthy tooth remains. An inlay or onlay can sit between the two when the damage is too large for a filling and the tooth doesn't need a crown.

What's removed The decayed or damaged tissue A small amount of enamel, to make room for the crown

Possible problems after a filling

Mild sensitivity is common and temporary. These are the problems to watch for and what they can mean.

  • Pain when you bite. The filling may be interfering with your bite, and it can be reshaped
  • A throbbing toothache. Decay may have reached the pulp, which may need a root canal
  • Pain from heat or cold that lingers. The nerve may be damaged, which may need a root canal or an extraction
  • A filling that fractures over time, one of the main reasons composite fillings fail
  • New decay around the filling, which often appears after three years or later

The ADA reports patient behavior as the most significant factor in how long a composite filling lasts. Daily brushing and flossing, a balanced diet and regular visits all help.

How much does a filling cost in Miami?

The price depends on the tooth and what it needs, so we give you a cost estimate after the exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • How large the cavity is and how much of the tooth it involves
  • Which tooth it is and how many teeth need treatment
  • Whether a filling is enough or the tooth needs an inlay, onlay or crown
  • What your insurance covers

We work with many dental plans, including Delta Dental, MetLife, Cigna, Aetna and Humana, and we check your coverage before you start. Coverage varies, and the ADA notes that some plans don't cover tooth-colored fillings. Original Medicare doesn't cover most dental care, though some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits. Dental filling questions

How long do fillings last?

Cleveland Clinic says fillings generally last 10 to 20 years, depending on the material. The ADA cites a 2012 study that found about 90% overall success for composite fillings at 10 years, and notes that some studies suggest composites may need replacing sooner than amalgam.

How long do dental restorations last?

Longevity varies with the material, bite, oral hygiene and regular care. No restoration lasts forever.

What is the difference between a crown and an inlay or onlay?

A crown covers most or all of the visible tooth; an inlay or onlay restores a smaller portion when sufficient healthy tooth structure remains.

Does getting a filling hurt?

We numb the tooth with local anesthesia, so it shouldn't hurt during treatment. Tell us if you feel any discomfort. Mild sensitivity or gum soreness afterward is normal and should fade within a week or two.

How long does a filling take?

A standard filling usually takes an hour or less, and a simple one can take about 20 minutes. Larger fillings or several in one visit take longer.

Can I eat after a filling?

Yes. Eating won't damage the filling, but waiting until the numbness wears off helps you avoid biting your cheek. If you eat sooner, chew slowly and carefully.

Can a cavity heal without a filling?

Only at the very start. Fluoride treatments can repair enamel in a cavity that's just beginning. Once a hole has formed, a filling is the usual treatment.

When is it too late for a filling?

If decay reaches the pulp inside the tooth, a filling won't fix it and you'll need a root canal. If too much of the tooth is damaged, an inlay, onlay or crown may be a better fit.

I have old silver fillings. Should I replace them?

An intact silver filling doesn't automatically need replacing. The FDA says the available evidence doesn't show that mercury from amalgam harms the general population, and it encourages other materials for higher-risk groups. If you're pregnant or have a related health concern, tell us and we'll check them with you.

Will my filling whiten along with my teeth?

No. Crowns, veneers and fillings do not whiten like natural teeth. If you're thinking about whitening, mention it at your evaluation so we can go over what to expect.

Do you do fillings for children?

Yes. We see children for examinations, cleanings, preventive care, fillings and extractions according to their needs, and we explain each step in a friendly way.
