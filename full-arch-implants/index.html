Full-Arch Restoration on Implants in Miami & Hialeah

When many or all teeth in an arch are missing, a fixed implant-supported restoration may be an option. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we assess your health, bone and bite to determine the number of implants and the restorative plan. We explain temporary and final restorations, cleaning, maintenance and the expected timeline for your case.

What is a full-arch restoration on implants?

It replaces the teeth of your upper or lower jaw with one connected set held by dental implants. There are two main types: a fixed hybrid, which you don't take out at home, and a removable overdenture, which you take out to clean. When both jaws are treated, you may hear it called full-mouth dental implants.

  1. 1Fixed arch of teeth One connected set for the whole jaw
  2. 2Gum-colored base Holds the teeth and replaces lost gum
  3. 3Connector Joins the arch to the implant
  4. 4Implant A post placed in your jawbone
  5. 5Jawbone Needs enough bone to hold the implants

Is a full-arch restoration right for you?

Your jawbone, medical history and preferences all count. Only an evaluation can tell, and some conditions mean treating something else first or choosing another plan.

Often worth evaluating

  • You're missing most or all of the teeth in an arch
  • Many of your remaining teeth are too damaged to keep
  • Your current denture slips or feels loose
  • You have enough jawbone, or a graft may be an option
  • You can keep up daily cleaning and regular maintenance visits

May need treatment first or a closer look

  • Active or untreated gum disease
  • Unmanaged diabetes
  • Severe jawbone loss
  • Bone disorders or some autoimmune diseases
  • Smoking or vaping, which may disrupt healing
  • Radiation treatment to the head or neck
  • Trouble keeping up with oral hygiene

Tell us about every medication and health condition, including bone medications such as bisphosphonates. If implants aren't a good fit for you right now, we'll explain why and go over other options, such as dentures.

How we plan it at Nova

We look at your health, gums, bone and bite, and we obtain imaging and records when indicated, including a 3D CT scan when it's clinically needed. That tells us how much bone you have and whether a graft should be part of the conversation.

Local anesthesia numbs the area, and nitrous oxide is the sedation option we offer if you're nervous. Before you decide, we go over the alternatives, the stages and the cost of your plan.

Held by implants

The restoration attaches to implants in your jaw, which helps it stay in place. A traditional denture rests on the gums and can slip.

Two designs to compare

A fixed hybrid stays in when you go home. An overdenture comes out for cleaning. We explain how each one fits your case.

A plan you understand

You'll know the proposed number of implants, whether you need grafts, and what your temporary and final restorations involve.

Fixed hybrid (All-on-X)

A fixed full-arch restoration on implants. You do not remove it at home; a dental professional removes it when appropriate. It requires cleaning beneath and around it, plus maintenance.

  • You may also hear it called a hybrid denture
  • Cleaning includes floss threaders and interproximal brushes underneath
  • The number of implants depends on your anatomy and the design

Removable overdenture

A denture that attaches to implants and that you remove for cleaning. It may improve retention depending on the case, and its attachments may need replacement over time.

  • You take it out to clean it and to clean around the implants
  • Worn attachments are the most common reason for maintenance
  • It can suit you if cleaning under a fixed arch would be hard

If you have limited bone

Limited bone doesn't always rule out implants. A graft may expand the options in some cases, and we assess your bone, health and restoration plan first.

  • Ridge augmentation: when the ridge that once supported your teeth has become too narrow or too low, it aims to rebuild volume so implants can be positioned appropriately
  • Sinus lift: if there is too little bone beneath the sinus above your upper back teeth, the sinus lining is gently raised to create space for a bone graft
  • Sometimes the graft and implants are placed together. In other cases, the graft heals first

How full-arch treatment works, step by step

  1. 1

    Evaluation

    We review your health and medications, examine your gums, bone and bite, and obtain imaging and records when indicated.

  2. 2

    Your plan

    We compare a fixed hybrid and a removable overdenture for your case, and explain the number of implants, the stages, the risks and the cost.

  3. 3

    Preparing your mouth

    Teeth that can't be kept are removed, and grafts are done if you need them. You may need a few months of healing before implants go in.

  4. 4

    Placing the implants

    The area is numbed and the implants are placed in your jawbone. If you're nervous, we can assess whether nitrous oxide may help you relax.

  5. 5

    Healing with a temporary

    Your jawbone needs time to fuse to the implants, and you wear a temporary restoration meanwhile. Fixed teeth on the same day are possible only in selected cases, when the implants are stable enough. That restoration is still a temporary.

  6. 6

    Final restoration and upkeep

    Once you've healed, the final restoration is attached and we show you how to clean it. Regular maintenance visits continue after that.

Recovery and how long the process takes

It depends on how you heal and on whether you need grafts, so we give you the expected timeline for your case. These ranges come from medical references and aren't a promise.

First few days
Slight pain and swelling are normal, and over-the-counter pain relievers can help. Many people return to routine activities in two to three days. Avoid strenuous exercise for at least 48 hours.
First week
Most people feel like themselves again within about a week. Follow the eating and cleaning instructions we give you.
While implants heal
Cleveland Clinic puts this at three to nine months, depending on how quickly your body heals.
If you have a graft
Cleveland Clinic says a graft needs at least three months and a large one may take nine to 12. After a sinus lift, the American Academy of Periodontology says bone usually develops for four to 12 months before implants.
After the final restoration
Clean it at least twice a day and keep your maintenance visits.

Fixed hybrid or removable overdenture?

Both attach to implants. They differ in who takes them out, how you clean them and the upkeep they need.

Fixed hybrid Removable overdenture
Taking it out Only a dental professional, when appropriate You do, for cleaning
Cleaning at home Brush at least twice a day with a soft brush and nonabrasive toothpaste, then clean beneath it with floss threaders and interproximal brushes Take it out and clean it as instructed, at least twice a day. Brush and floss around the implants too
Maintenance Regular visits. A professional may remove it to check and clean it Regular visits. The attachments may need replacement over time
May suit you if You prefer teeth that stay in and can keep up with cleaning underneath You'd rather take it out to clean, or cleaning under a fixed arch would be hard for you

Risks and what can go wrong

Implant surgery and the restoration on top each carry their own risks. We review yours before you decide, and no result can be guaranteed.

  • Infection, nerve damage, injury inside the mouth or a hole in the sinus lining
  • An implant can fail to fuse with the bone or can be lost later
  • Inflammation around the implants can lead to bone loss, known as peri-implantitis. Smoking, poorly controlled diabetes, a history of gum disease, poor hygiene and missed maintenance raise the risk
  • Parts can loosen or break. In a study of 261 full-arch restorations followed for an average of 28.5 months, screw loosening was the most frequent problem, at 4.2%, followed by chipped or fractured material
  • Overdenture attachments wear and lose grip. They may need activating or replacing
  • A bone graft can fail, especially in people who smoke or have certain medical conditions

If an implant feels loose, your gums swell or something on the restoration breaks, call us so we can check it. Maintenance visits also give us a chance to look for these problems.

How much do full-arch dental implants cost in Miami?

The cost depends on your plan, so we go over it after your evaluation and before you decide. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • A fixed hybrid or a removable overdenture
  • One arch or both
  • The number of implants your anatomy and design call for
  • Teeth that need to be removed first
  • Grafts, ridge augmentation or a sinus lift, if you need them
  • Imaging and records
  • Your temporary and final restorations
  • Your plan's benefits

We accept a range of PPO dental insurance and Medicare Advantage plans with dental benefits, and we verify your specific plan, network and benefits before your visit. Full-arch implant questions

Is the fixed hybrid removable at home?

No, only by a dental professional when appropriate.

Can I get same-day teeth?

Possible in selected cases, not guaranteed.

How is a fixed hybrid cleaned?

It requires cleaning beneath and around it plus maintenance.

How long does it last?

It depends on hygiene, bite and maintenance.

How is it different from an overdenture?

A hybrid is fixed; an overdenture is removable.

Is an overdenture removable?

Yes, for cleaning.

How many implants do I need?

It depends on anatomy and design.

Does an overdenture need maintenance?

Yes, attachments may need replacement.

Is it more stable?

It may improve retention depending on the case.

How is an overdenture cleaned?

Remove it and clean it as instructed.

Is All-on-X the same as All-on-4?

No. All-on-4 is one method that uses four implants to replace an arch. Your number of implants depends on your anatomy and the design, so we don't assume four.

Does limited bone mean I cannot have implants?

Not necessarily. Bone grafting may expand the options in some cases. We assess your bone, health and restoration plan to explain suitable alternatives.
