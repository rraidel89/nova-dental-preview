Dental Implants in Miami & Hialeah

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth, so the new tooth stays fixed and you can chew normally again. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we offer dental implants to patients from all over Miami. We plan each implant, place it and fit the final crown in our office.

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small post, usually made of titanium, that takes the place of a tooth root. It goes into the jawbone, and over a few months the bone grows around it. A connector called an abutment then holds a custom crown, a bridge or a denture on top.

  1. 1Crown The part you see and chew with
  2. 2Abutment Joins the crown to the implant
  3. 3Implant The post that replaces the root
  4. 4Jawbone Grows around the post in three to nine months

Is a dental implant right for you?

Most adults who have lost a tooth can get an implant. The first things we check are whether there's enough healthy bone to hold the post and whether your gums are healthy enough to heal well.

Usually a good fit

  • You're missing one tooth, several or a full arch
  • Your jaw has finished growing, usually by about 18. There is no upper age limit
  • Your gums are healthy, or you're willing to treat gum disease first
  • You'd rather not take your teeth out at night

May need treatment or a closer look first

  • You smoke or vape, which can get in the way of healing
  • Active gum disease or a lot of tooth decay
  • Severe bone loss in the jaw. A bone graft can sometimes rebuild it
  • Diabetes that isn't under control
  • Some bone disorders or autoimmune conditions, or the medicines used for them

We go through each of these at your consultation. If an implant isn't the right call for you, we'll tell you and show you the other options.

How we plan your implant

Before surgery, we plan the implant digitally. Studying your case this way lets us choose the implant's position, angle and length ahead of time.

With 3D guided technology, a custom guide carries that plan into your mouth, so the implant goes where it was planned.

Stays in place

An implant doesn't slip or come out. You brush and floss it like your other teeth.

Leaves your other teeth alone

A traditional bridge needs the teeth on each side filed down. An implant stands on its own.

A long track record

A 2019 review of long-term studies found about 96 in 100 implants still in place after 10 years.

The dental implant process, step by step

  1. 1

    Consultation and plan

    We examine your mouth, take images of your jaw and go over your options and costs. You leave with a personalized treatment plan.

  2. 2

    Getting the site ready

    If the tooth is still there, it comes out first. If there isn't enough bone, a bone graft may come before the implant and add some healing time.

  3. 3

    Placing the implant

    The area is numbed and the post goes into the jawbone. You shouldn't feel pain during the procedure.

  4. 4

    Healing

    Over the next three to nine months the bone fuses with the implant. If the gap shows when you smile, you wear a temporary tooth in the meantime.

  5. 5

    Your new tooth

    Once the implant is solid, we attach the abutment and your custom crown, bridge or denture, matched to your other teeth.

Dental implant recovery, day by day

The first week is the part you'll notice. After that, most of the healing happens in the bone, out of sight.

Day of surgery
Your mouth stays numb for a few hours. Rest, hold ice on your cheek and stick to soft, cool foods.
First few days
Some soreness and swelling are normal. Over-the-counter pain relievers usually take care of it.
First week
The gums do most of their first healing, and most people feel like themselves again within a week or so.
Months 3 to 9
The bone fuses with the implant. You won't feel it happen, so we check it before placing your final tooth.

Call us right away if you have a fever, pus or bleeding around the implant, swollen gums, or an implant that feels loose.

Risks and downsides of dental implants

Implants have a strong track record, but they involve surgery, and you should know the downsides before you decide.

  • It takes time. Going from placement to your final tooth usually takes several months.
  • It costs more upfront than a bridge or a denture.
  • Implants can fail. In that 2019 review, about 96 in 100 implants were still in place after 10 years, and about 93 in 100 in its most cautious analysis.
  • Infection of the gum and bone around an implant, called peri-implantitis, is the main long-term problem. A 2025 review of 102 studies found it in about 1 in 5 patients. Smoking, diabetes and past gum disease make it more likely.
  • As with any surgery, there are risks: infection, damage to nearby teeth or blood vessels, nerve damage that causes numbness or tingling, and sinus problems with some upper implants. Mayo Clinic describes these risks as small and usually easy to treat.

To keep an implant healthy, brush and floss it every day and come in for regular cleanings.

How much do dental implants cost in Miami?

The price depends on what your case needs, so we give you a cost estimate after your exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • How many teeth you're replacing, and whether with a crown, a bridge or a full arch
  • Whether a tooth has to come out first
  • Whether you need a bone graft
  • The material of the crown or the prosthesis
  • What your insurance covers

Dental implant questions

How long do dental implants last?

With good care, the implant itself can often last a lifetime. The crown on top wears like any crown and usually lasts around 15 years. A 2019 review of long-term studies found about 96 in 100 implants still in place after 10 years.

Do dental implants hurt?

You shouldn't feel pain during the procedure because the area is numbed. Afterward, mild soreness and swelling are normal for a few days, and over-the-counter pain relievers usually handle it. If the pain gets worse instead of better, call us.

Does insurance cover dental implants?

It depends on your plan. Some dental plans pay part of the cost, some cover only the crown and some exclude implants. We verify your benefits before treatment, so you know your share before you commit.

Does Medicare cover dental implants?

Original Medicare, Parts A and B, doesn't cover dental implants or dentures. Some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits that may help. Check your plan's coverage, or bring your card and we'll look it up with you.

What can stop you from getting dental implants?

Few things rule you out for good. Smoking, untreated gum disease, uncontrolled diabetes, severe bone loss or a jaw that's still growing can delay treatment or make it riskier. Many of these can be treated first, and we'll tell you where you stand at your consultation.

How long is recovery after implant surgery?

Take it easy for the first day or two and eat soft foods. Most people feel like themselves again within a week or so. The bone keeps fusing with the implant for three to nine months, but you won't notice it.

What happens if I lose a tooth and don't replace it?

The bone that held the tooth starts to shrink. One study found the bone ridge lost about half its width within a year of an extraction, most of it in the first three months. The teeth next to the gap can also drift over time.
