Bone Grafts and Sinus Lift in Miami & Hialeah

If you have lost a tooth or have been told you have limited bone, Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah can assess your options for restoring the area. A bone graft adds material that helps preserve or rebuild bone and provides support for new bone formation. Whether you need one depends on your anatomy and the treatment being considered.

What is a dental bone graft?

A dental bone graft places material where the jawbone is thin or missing, so the area can support future treatment such as an implant. It's most often discussed after an extraction, or when an implant is planned and the bone looks too narrow, too low or too close to the sinus. A sinus lift is one type of graft, done in the back of the upper jaw.

  1. 1Maxillary sinus Air-filled space above the upper back teeth
  2. 2Raised sinus lining Gently lifted to create space
  3. 3Bone graft Fills the space under the lining
  4. 4Existing bone Your jawbone, which may be too low for an implant

Do you need a bone graft?

It depends on your bone, your health and the treatment you're considering. Many people ask about it for an implant, but a graft doesn't mean an implant is right for everyone.

A graft may be worth discussing if

  • You're having a tooth removed and thinking about what will replace it
  • You've been told you don't have enough bone for an implant
  • A tooth has been missing for some time and the ridge has become narrow or low
  • You've lost upper back teeth and there's little bone under the sinus
  • You lost bone because of gum disease, an injury or an infection

May need a closer look first

  • A history of sinus problems, or a nose that often feels blocked
  • Smoking or vaping, which raises the chance of the graft failing
  • Medical conditions that can affect healing
  • Medications you take regularly, so we can review them with you
  • A flight coming up soon after a sinus lift

Limited bone doesn't automatically rule out an implant, and a graft doesn't make anyone a candidate on its own. If a graft isn't right for you, we'll explain why and talk through the alternatives.

How we plan it at Nova

Before treatment, we review your health, medications and any necessary imaging, including 3D imaging when it's indicated. For a sinus lift, we also assess the available bone and the health of the sinus.

We explain the procedure, its benefits, risks and alternatives, along with the technique and material we suggest for your case. Aftercare and follow-up are part of that conversation, and so is any referral your case may call for.

More options in some cases

Limited bone doesn't always mean no implant. A graft may expand your options, depending on your bone, health and restoration plan.

Support where the tooth will go

Rebuilding volume can help an implant be positioned appropriately for your future smile and bite.

A plan you understand first

You hear the stages, the alternatives and the costs before you decide, and we check your progress before each next stage.

Preserving bone after an extraction

After a tooth is removed, the surrounding bone changes and may lose volume. In selected cases, placing a graft in the tooth socket helps limit this loss and prepare the area for future restoration. Not every extraction requires a bone graft.

This is often called socket preservation. A Cochrane review found it may reduce bone loss, though the evidence is very uncertain, and it found no clear best material or technique.

  • Done at the time of the extraction or planned around it
  • May limit bone loss, without stopping every change

Ridge augmentation

When the ridge that once supported your teeth has become too narrow or too low, ridge augmentation may be considered. The aim is to rebuild volume so implants can be positioned appropriately for your future smile and bite. The approach depends on the shape and extent of the bone loss.

  • Often comes up when a tooth has been missing for some time
  • We explain the technique and material for your case

Sinus lift

The maxillary sinus is an air-filled space above the upper back teeth. If there is too little bone beneath it, a sinus lift may help prepare the area for implants. The procedure gently raises the sinus lining to create space for a bone graft. At Nova, we assess the available bone and sinus health before recommending this treatment.

  • Only applies to the back of the upper jaw
  • Comes with extra care for your nose and sinus while you heal

How a bone graft is done, step by step

  1. 1

    Evaluation

    We review your health, medications and imaging, and check how much bone you have and where the implant or other restoration would go.

  2. 2

    Your plan

    We tell you whether a graft makes sense, which type, whether the implant can go in at the same time, and what the alternatives are.

  3. 3

    Numbing

    We numb the area with local anesthesia. If the visit makes you anxious, we can assess whether nitrous oxide may help you relax. It's the sedation option we offer.

  4. 4

    Placing the graft

    The gum is opened and the area cleaned, then the graft is placed. For a sinus lift, the sinus lining is gently raised first to make room. The graft may be covered with a protective membrane before the gum is closed with stitches.

  5. 5

    Healing and follow-up

    We give you your aftercare instructions and see you for follow-up visits. We assess your progress before moving to the next stage.

Recovery after a bone graft or sinus lift

Gums and bone heal at different rates, and the timeline depends on the procedure and on you. Cleveland Clinic describes initial recovery of about a week. The instructions we give you for your case come first.

First day or two
Some oozing is normal. Use ice on your cheek or jaw, keep your head raised, and avoid spitting or swishing hard.
First week or two
Tenderness, swelling and bruising are common and usually settle in a week or two. Swelling tends to peak around day 2 or 3. Eat soft foods for the first few days, and don't disturb the area.
Small grains
You may notice small grains of graft material in your mouth during the first days. That's usually normal. Tell us if there's a large amount.
Habits and activity
Don't smoke or vape. Cleveland Clinic advises no alcohol for at least two weeks, and no exercise or heavy lifting for at least 48 hours. We may ask you to wait longer.
After a sinus lift
Don't blow your nose, and wipe it gently instead. Sneeze with your mouth open and avoid straws. One university protocol asks for this for at least a week, and no air travel for at least two. Some sinus drainage or light nasal bleeding can happen.
The following months
Graft maturation usually takes several months. Cleveland Clinic cites at least three months, and nine to 12 for large grafts. We assess your progress before the next stage.

Call us at 786-687-0909 if you have a fever of 101°F or higher, pus or a foul taste, severe pain, swelling or pain that gets worse after the first days, bleeding that doesn't stop with pressure, air passing through the area, or if you think the graft has moved. If you get hives or itching from a medication, stop taking it and call. If you have trouble breathing, call 911.

Graft first, or graft and implant together?

Sometimes the graft and implant can be placed together. In other cases, the graft is placed first and allowed to heal. A 2025 review of sinus lifts found comparable implant survival with both approaches, though it noted that the evidence is limited.

Graft before the implant Graft and implant together
When it may fit When there's too little bone for the implant to be stable at placement When the available bone can hold the implant stable from the start
Procedures Usually one for the graft and a later one for the implant Usually one for both
Waiting The graft heals first, usually for several months No separate wait before the implant, but healing still comes before the final tooth
Checking progress We assess the graft before placing the implant We assess how the area heals before the next stage

Bone graft and sinus lift risks

Most swelling and soreness passes with time, but this is surgery and it carries risks. We go over the ones that apply to your case before you decide.

  • Infection, nerve damage, heavy bleeding or complications from anesthesia, which Cleveland Clinic lists as possible risks
  • Graft failure, more likely in people who smoke or have certain medical conditions. In some cases the procedure has to be repeated
  • A tear in the sinus lining during a sinus lift. It's usually repaired at the time, and in complicated cases the procedure may be paused so the lining can heal
  • Sinus inflammation after a sinus lift. A 2023 review in the Ochsner Journal reports acute sinusitis in 10% to 20% of patients and chronic symptoms in 4% to 8%, with more risk after a tear, with tobacco use or with past sinus disease
  • Graft material moving out of place, or an opening between the sinus and the mouth
  • Less bone than planned, which can change the next steps of your plan

Smoking and sinus history are two things worth telling us about early. If your sinus history raises questions, we may suggest an evaluation by an ear, nose and throat doctor before going ahead.

Bone graft and sinus lift questions

Does limited bone mean I cannot have implants?

Not necessarily. Bone grafting may expand the options in some cases. We assess your bone, health and restoration plan to explain suitable alternatives.

Can the graft and implant be placed together?

Sometimes. In other cases, the graft is placed first and allowed to heal. The decision depends on factors including the available bone and whether the implant can be stable when it is placed.

How long does healing take?

Gums and bone heal at different rates. Graft maturation usually takes several months. We explain the expected timeline, aftercare and follow-up visits, and assess your progress before moving to the next stage.

Is grafting always needed?

No, it depends on bone and planning.

Do you offer gum grafting?

Yes, when clinically indicated.

What is a sinus lift?

It helps increase bone support in the back upper jaw. The sinus lining is gently raised to create space for a bone graft, which can help prepare the area for implants.

What's the difference between socket preservation, ridge augmentation and a sinus lift?

Socket preservation places a graft in the socket of a removed tooth. Ridge augmentation rebuilds a ridge that has become too narrow or too low. A sinus lift adds bone under the sinus in the back of the upper jaw.

Does a bone graft hurt?

We use local anesthesia during the procedure, and recovery varies. Tenderness and swelling are common afterward, and over-the-counter pain relievers or medication we prescribe can help. Call us if the pain gets worse instead of better.

Can I blow my nose or fly after a sinus lift?

You should avoid blowing your nose and flying while the area heals, because sinus pressure can disturb the graft. One university protocol advises at least one week without blowing your nose and at least two without air travel. We'll tell you what applies to you.

How soon after the graft can I get the implant?

We assess your progress first, because the timing varies. Cleveland Clinic advises getting the implant within six to 12 months once the graft has healed, since a graft can shrink and lose density if you wait too long.

Is the graft included in the implant package?

No. Our single-tooth implant package covers the CT scan, implant, custom abutment and crown. Additional procedures, including grafts, are quoted separately, and we explain them before you decide.
