Teeth Whitening in Miami & Hialeah

Coffee, tea, wine and time all darken teeth. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we offer professional teeth whitening to patients from all over Miami, with a professional gel and LED light, after checking that your teeth and gums are ready for it.

How does teeth whitening work?

Whitening gels use peroxide to break up the stains inside and on the surface of your teeth. Hydrogen peroxide works faster but is more likely to cause sensitivity, while carbamide peroxide works more slowly and gently. Whitening only lightens natural enamel. Crowns, veneers and fillings keep their color.

  1. 1Enamel The outer layer the gel goes through
  2. 2Surface stains From coffee, tea, wine or tobacco
  3. 3Pulp The living center of the tooth
  4. 4Deeper discoloration Inside the dentin, harder to lift

Is whitening right for you?

Whitening works best on healthy teeth that have darkened with time or from food and drinks.

Usually a good fit

  • Yellowing from coffee, tea, red wine or tobacco
  • Teeth that have darkened with age
  • You want a brighter smile before an event
  • You're planning veneers or crowns and want your natural teeth lighter first

May need treatment or another option first

  • Cavities or gum disease, which we treat before whitening
  • Visible crowns, veneers or fillings, which won't change color
  • Very sensitive teeth. We may adjust the gel or the plan
  • Stains that don't respond to whitening. Veneers may be the answer

We check your teeth before whitening so the result is even and your gums are protected.

After whitening

There's no downtime, but a few habits help the result last.

First days
Some sensitivity is common and passes. A toothpaste for sensitive teeth helps.
First 48 hours
Go easy on coffee, tea, red wine and other dark foods and drinks.
Every day
Brush and floss daily, don't smoke and limit drinks that stain.
Over time
Teeth darken again little by little. A touch-up keeps them bright.

Downsides of whitening

Whitening is a safe, common treatment, but it has limits and side effects.

  • Sensitivity is common with every whitening method. It's usually temporary.
  • Crowns, veneers and fillings keep their color, so they can stand out after whitening.
  • Results aren't permanent. Coffee, tea, red wine and aging darken teeth again.
  • Studies show bleaching can cause small changes on the enamel surface, which is why more isn't better.

Whitening under a dentist's care lets us adjust the plan to your teeth.

Teeth whitening questions

How long does teeth whitening last?

It isn't permanent. Coffee, tea, red wine, smoking and aging darken teeth again over time. Brushing, flossing and limiting staining drinks help it last, and a touch-up brings the shade back.

Is it worth getting teeth whitened at the dentist?

At the office we check your teeth first, protect your gums and adjust the plan if you're sensitive. We can also tell you if old fillings or crowns will stand out after whitening.

Does whitening damage enamel?

Used properly, whitening is safe. Studies show bleaching can cause small changes on the enamel surface, so we don't recommend overdoing it, and sensitivity is common but usually temporary.

Which teeth can't be whitened?

Crowns, veneers and fillings don't change color. Some deep stains also respond less. If whitening won't give you the result you want, veneers may be an option.

Is there a downside to teeth whitening?

The main one is sensitivity, which happens with every whitening method and usually passes in a few days. Results also fade over time.

Do teeth turn yellow again after whitening?

Gradually, yes. How fast depends on what you eat and drink and whether you smoke. A touch-up keeps them bright.

Does insurance cover teeth whitening?

Usually not, because it's considered cosmetic. The exam and cleaning you may need before whitening are often covered.
