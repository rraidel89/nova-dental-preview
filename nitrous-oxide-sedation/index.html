Nitrous Oxide Sedation in Miami & Hialeah

If treatment makes you nervous, tell us what concerns you. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we can assess whether nitrous oxide mixed with oxygen may help you relax. You breathe it through a small mask over your nose and remain awake, able to communicate with the team. Local anesthesia is used when the procedure requires it. We review your health and explain benefits, risks and alternatives before you decide.

What is nitrous oxide?

Nitrous oxide, often called laughing gas, is a gas you breathe mixed with oxygen to feel calmer during dental treatment. You stay awake and can answer us. It takes effect quickly and clears through your breath within minutes once the gas is turned off.

  1. 1Nose mask Rests over your nose only
  2. 2Your mouth Stays free so we can work and you can talk
  3. 3Airway The gas reaches your lungs as you breathe normally
  4. 4Tubing Brings the gas and oxygen, and carries away what you exhale

Is nitrous oxide right for you?

It suits many people who feel anxious at the dentist, but your health history decides. We go through it with you first, and some conditions mean waiting or choosing another plan.

Often a good fit

  • Dental visits make you nervous, or you've put off care because of it
  • You have a strong gag reflex
  • You find it hard to sit through longer appointments
  • You breathe comfortably through your nose
  • You'd like to stay awake and able to respond

May need to wait or a closer look

  • A cold, sinus infection or allergies that block your nose
  • The first trimester of pregnancy
  • COPD or another serious lung condition
  • Untreated vitamin B12 deficiency
  • A recent ear infection, or ear, nose or throat surgery in the last two weeks
  • Recent eye surgery or raised eye pressure
  • Bleomycin treatment, or a history of substance dependence

Tell us about every medication and health condition, even if it seems unrelated. If nitrous oxide isn't the right choice for you, we'll explain why and talk through other ways to make your visit easier.

How we use it at Nova

Nitrous oxide is the sedation option we offer. It helps you relax, and local anesthesia numbs the area when the procedure requires it.

We can talk it over before cleanings and fillings as well as extractions and other longer procedures, based on your health and how you feel about the visit.

You stay awake

You can hear us and answer us throughout, and we can check how you're doing as we go.

Works and clears quickly

It starts within minutes of breathing it in. Once it stops, the gas leaves your body through your breath.

Delivered with oxygen

It's always mixed with oxygen. According to the ADA, these systems include built-in safety features and normally deliver at least 30% oxygen.

What a visit with nitrous oxide is like

  1. 1

    We review your health

    We ask about your conditions, medications and any pregnancy, explain the benefits, risks and alternatives, and answer your questions.

  2. 2

    The mask goes on

    A small mask rests over your nose. You breathe normally through your nose and try not to talk too much, so the mixture works evenly.

  3. 3

    You begin to relax

    Most people feel calmer within a few minutes. You may notice warmth, tingling in your hands or feet, or feel a little light. Tell us how you feel, because the amount can be adjusted.

  4. 4

    Your treatment

    If the procedure requires it, we numb the area with local anesthesia. Nitrous oxide doesn't replace it.

  5. 5

    Oxygen at the end

    We turn off the gas and you breathe oxygen for at least five minutes, which helps clear what's left and can prevent a headache.

How long does nitrous oxide take to wear off?

Usually minutes. Before you leave, we make sure you feel the way you did when you came in.

When the gas stops
You breathe oxygen for at least five minutes while the effect fades.
Before you leave
We check that you're fully alert. Take your time and don't rush to stand up.
Driving
Many people can drive themselves once fully alert, and the California Dental Association estimates that takes about 15 minutes. If you don't feel back to normal, tell us and don't drive.
The rest of the day
Eat lightly at first, and follow the separate care instructions for your treatment. If you were numbed, avoid chewing on that side until the feeling returns.

Wheezing, trouble breathing, hives or a swollen face after your visit need emergency care: call 911. If nausea, headache or dizziness doesn't go away, call us.

Nitrous oxide or local anesthesia?

They do different jobs, and many treatments use both. One helps with nerves and the other blocks pain in one area.

Nitrous oxide Local anesthesia
What it does Helps you relax Numbs the area being treated
How you get it You breathe it through a nose mask A small injection, usually after a numbing gel
Are you awake? Yes, and able to respond Yes, fully
How long it lasts Fades within minutes of stopping Often a few hours
Pain relief afterward None Until the numbness wears off

Is nitrous oxide safe? Side effects to know

The American Dental Association describes it as safe and effective when used appropriately. Side effects are usually short-lived, and reviewing your health first lowers the risk.

  • Nausea or vomiting, the most common effects. The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry reports them in about 0.5% to 1.2% of patients, more often with longer use or after a heavy meal
  • Headache or dizziness, often when the gas stops. Oxygen at the end of the visit helps
  • Sweating, restlessness or feeling uneasy. Tell us, because we can lower the amount or stop
  • Feeling too drowsy, which is why we keep talking with you during treatment
  • Problems for people with untreated vitamin B12 deficiency, and for those with the conditions listed above

If anything feels wrong during treatment, say so or raise your hand. Removing the mask is quick, and the effect starts to fade right away.

Before your visit

A little preparation helps your appointment go smoothly. If something changes on the day, call us before you come in.

  • Bring a list of your medications and health conditions
  • Tell us if you are or might be pregnant
  • Call us if you have a cold, a blocked nose or an ear infection, because we may need to reschedule
  • Eat lightly beforehand. You don't need to fast for nitrous oxide alone, but a heavy meal raises the chance of nausea
  • Tell us what makes you nervous, such as the sounds, the needle or feeling out of control

Nitrous oxide questions

Will I be asleep?

No. You remain awake and can communicate with the team. You should feel calmer, and some people feel a little drowsy, but you can still respond.

Is nitrous oxide safe?

The American Dental Association describes it as safe and effective when used appropriately. It isn't right for everyone, so we review your health and medications before suggesting it.

How long does it take to wear off?

Usually minutes. When the gas stops, you breathe oxygen for at least five minutes, and we confirm you're alert before you go.

Can I drive after nitrous oxide?

Many people can once they're fully alert, which the California Dental Association estimates takes about 15 minutes. We'll check on you first. If you don't feel normal, don't drive.

Does it stop the pain?

It helps you relax and may ease discomfort, but it doesn't replace local anesthesia, and it doesn't relieve pain after treatment.

Can I eat before my appointment?

Yes. Fasting isn't required for nitrous oxide on its own, but stick to a light meal, since a heavy one makes nausea more likely.

Can I have it if I'm pregnant?

Tell us if you are or might be pregnant. Nitrous oxide is usually avoided in the first trimester, and later in pregnancy we'd talk with your obstetrician before considering it.

I have asthma. Can I still have it?

Asthma doesn't automatically rule it out. COPD and other serious lung conditions need a closer look. Tell us about your breathing and bring your inhaler.

Can my child have nitrous oxide?

It's used with some children, and the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry has guidance on it, but sedation isn't a routine part of children's care with us. If your child feels very anxious, call us and we'll talk through the options.

Do you offer IV sedation?

No. Nitrous oxide is the sedation option we offer at Nova. If your case calls for something different, we'll tell you and explain your options.
