Facial Aesthetics and Bruxism Care in Miami & Hialeah

Do expression lines concern you, or do you clench or grind your teeth and wake up with a sore jaw? At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, these are treatment options we evaluate case by case, with the expected effect, alternatives and risks explained before you decide. Your goals and health history guide the conversation, and no option comes with a guaranteed result.

What are botulinum toxin, bruxism care and facial PRF?

Botulinum toxin is a medicine injected into a chosen muscle. It blocks the nerve signal that makes the muscle contract, and the effect is temporary. Around the forehead and eyes, it can soften lines that form with expression. Bruxism is clenching or grinding your teeth, awake or asleep, and in selected cases the toxin may be used in the jaw muscles. Facial PRF is a separate option, prepared from a sample of your own blood.

  1. 1Forehead muscles Their movement forms forehead and frown lines
  2. 2Temporalis muscle Helps close the jaw, at the side of your head
  3. 3Masseter muscle A main chewing muscle, at the angle of the jaw
  4. 4Teeth Grinding can leave wear or cracks here

Could one of these options suit you?

It depends on what concerns you and on your health history. We go through both with you, and some conditions mean waiting or choosing another plan.

Worth talking over with us

  • Forehead lines, frown lines, crow's feet or lines along the sides of your nose concern you
  • You clench or grind your teeth, awake or during sleep
  • You wake up with sore jaw muscles, facial pain or a headache
  • Your teeth look worn, or you've cracked a tooth
  • You'd like to know whether facial PRF makes sense for you, including the limits of the evidence

May need to wait or a closer look

  • An allergy to any botulinum toxin product or its ingredients
  • An infection where the injection would go
  • Pregnancy, plans to become pregnant, or breastfeeding
  • Myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton syndrome, ALS or another nerve or muscle disease
  • Breathing or swallowing problems, now or in the past
  • Drooping eyelids, weak forehead muscles or past surgery on your face
  • Heart disease, bleeding problems, or blood thinners or aspirin-like medicines
  • Botulinum toxin in the last four months, a recent antibiotic by injection, muscle relaxants, or allergy, cold or sleep medicines

Tell us about every medication and health condition, even if it seems unrelated. We review your health before facial PRF as well. If an option isn't right for you, we'll explain why and talk through the alternatives.

How we approach it at Nova

Facial aesthetics and bruxism have different goals, so we evaluate them separately. For lines, we start with what you'd like to change. For clenching or grinding, we start with your teeth, your discomfort and what may be contributing.

Some uses we discuss are off-label in the US, which means the FDA labeling we reviewed doesn't list them. We tell you when that applies, what the evidence shows and what remains uncertain.

A temporary effect

The effect of botulinum toxin usually lasts about 3 to 4 months, so you can reassess before deciding on another treatment.

Aimed at chosen muscles

The medicine goes into selected muscles, based on your evaluation and on what you'd like to address.

More than one route

Bruxism care can also include a night guard, stress reduction and changes to caffeine, alcohol or smoking. We go over what applies to you.

Botulinum toxin for facial aesthetics

If expression lines concern you, we assess your goals and whether this treatment may be appropriate. Nova evaluates forehead lines, frown lines, crow’s feet and lines along the sides of the nose. We explain the expected temporary effect, alternatives, possible adverse effects and aftercare before you decide.

  • The FDA labeling we reviewed lists moderate to severe forehead lines, frown lines and crow's feet in adults
  • Lines along the sides of the nose aren't listed there, so treating them is off-label
  • Approved uses differ between products, and doses can't be converted from one product to another

Bruxism and muscle tension

If you clench or grind your teeth, we assess tooth wear, discomfort and contributing factors. In selected cases, botulinum toxin in the masseter and temporalis muscles may temporarily reduce clenching force and help with muscle discomfort. It may complement other care; it does not cure bruxism or replace dental protection and follow-up when needed.

  • This use is off-label in the US: bruxism isn't among the uses on the FDA labeling we reviewed
  • Trials are few and small. Reviews report less pain and lower contraction force, and they disagree on whether grinding episodes become fewer
  • A 2017 review found studies on sleep bruxism only, none on clenching while awake

Facial PRF

PRF is prepared from a sample of your own blood. At Nova, we assess suitability for facial use and discuss your goals, limits of the evidence, aftercare and risks of the proposed technique. Suitability and results are individual.

  • The research is mixed, with small studies, and many have no control group
  • A 2025 review that pooled PRF with a related product, PRP, found more consistent results for skin thickness and elasticity than for wrinkles, and the weakest for hydration
  • We don't promise rejuvenation or wrinkle removal

How your evaluation and treatment work

  1. 1

    Tell us your concern

    Lines you'd like to soften, a sore jaw, worn teeth or curiosity about PRF. Tell us what prompted the visit and what you hope for.

  2. 2

    We evaluate you

    We ask about your health and medications. If you clench or grind, we look at tooth wear, discomfort and contributing factors.

  3. 3

    We explain your options

    We go over the expected effect, alternatives, possible adverse effects, aftercare and cost, and whether the use is off-label. You can take time to decide.

  4. 4

    If you decide to go ahead

    Botulinum toxin is injected into the chosen muscles in a brief outpatient visit, and you go home the same day. For PRF, a blood sample is drawn and spun in a centrifuge, and we explain the proposed technique first.

  5. 5

    Follow-up

    We review how you responded and any side effects. For bruxism, that includes whether your teeth still need protection.

Recovery, and how long botulinum toxin lasts

Most people can return to work and most activities right away, unless we tell you otherwise. Cleveland Clinic gives these aftercare times, and we'll confirm yours.

First 3 to 4 hours
Stay upright, and don't lie down.
First 12 hours
Don't rub or press on the treated area.
First 24 hours
Avoid physical exertion.
First 2 weeks
Most people notice the effect around day 3 or 4, and the full effect within 10 to 14 days. In the jaw muscles, it can show anywhere from a day to a couple of weeks.
About 3 to 4 months
The effect fades, and we can reassess. Dosing more often than every 3 months hasn't been clinically evaluated, according to the FDA labeling for cosmetic use.
After facial PRF
Aftercare depends on the technique. In a chart review of 50 people treated with a fibrin matrix, most had minor bruising for 1 to 3 days, and no one's swelling lasted more than 5 days.

Trouble breathing, swallowing or speaking, wheezing, or swelling of your face, lips or throat needs emergency care: call 911. These effects can appear hours to weeks after botulinum toxin. If you notice muscle weakness, vision changes or dizziness, don't drive, and call us at 786-687-0909.

Risks and side effects of botulinum toxin and PRF

Most side effects are mild and short-lived, but serious ones are possible. We go over the ones that apply to your treatment before you decide.

  • Pain, swelling, redness or bruising where the needle went in, headache, neck pain or flu-like symptoms. Cleveland Clinic says most improve within a day or two
  • Drooping eyelid or brow, dry or irritated eyes, or blurred or double vision. For one product, FDA labeling reports drooping eyelid in 3% of people treated for frown lines, and headache in 9% and drooping brow in 2% of those treated for forehead lines
  • Spread of the effect beyond the injection site, the subject of an FDA boxed warning for all botulinum toxin products. Trouble swallowing or breathing can be life-threatening, and deaths have been reported. The same labeling says no definitive serious reports of this kind have been linked to labeled cosmetic doses
  • Serious reactions in unapproved uses, including excessive weakness, trouble swallowing and aspiration pneumonia, some of them fatal. The labeling says safety and effectiveness for unapproved uses haven't been established
  • In the jaw muscles: a change in your smile or bulging of the muscle. In a chart review of 80 people, 18.8% had a side effect, including smile asymmetry in 2.5% and bulging in 2.5%
  • Weaker chewing and smaller muscles. In a trial of people with jaw muscle pain, chewing performance dropped for a time, and muscle thickness and jaw bone volume fell with dose. The long-term effect of repeated treatment isn't well studied
  • Allergic reactions such as itching, rash, hives or wheezing
  • For facial PRF, bruising and swelling. A 2025 review reports no serious events in the studies it included, but those studies are small and the techniques vary

If you've had a side effect from botulinum toxin before, tell us and tell us which product it was. These products can't be used interchangeably.

Questions about bruxism treatment and facial aesthetics

Can botulinum toxin help with teeth grinding?

In selected cases, it may temporarily reduce clenching force and help with muscle discomfort. The trials are few and small, and reviews disagree on whether grinding episodes become fewer. Cleveland Clinic describes it as an option for severe cases.

Is botulinum toxin for bruxism FDA approved?

Bruxism isn't among the uses on the FDA labeling we reviewed, so this use is off-label in the US. We can discuss it as an option, and we explain what that means for you before you decide.

What are masseter injections?

The masseter is a main chewing muscle at the angle of your jaw. For bruxism, botulinum toxin may be injected into it and, in some cases, into the temporalis at the side of your head, to reduce how hard those muscles contract for a while.

How long does it last?

Usually about 3 to 4 months, and then the muscles regain strength. To keep the effect you would need another treatment, which is something we reassess with you each time.

Does it cure bruxism, and do I still need a night guard?

It doesn't cure bruxism or replace dental protection. Whether you need a night guard depends on your tooth wear and your evaluation, so it's an option we discuss with you.

Is bruxism the same as a TMJ disorder?

No. Bruxism is clenching or grinding your teeth. TMJ disorders involve the jaw joint and the muscles around it, and grinding can contribute to them. We evaluate them separately, and the evidence for one doesn't automatically apply to the other.

What else can help with bruxism?

Cleveland Clinic lists a custom mouth guard, stress reduction, cutting back on caffeine or alcohol and quitting smoking, among others. Stress, anxiety, some medications and sleep apnea have been linked to grinding, so tell us about them.

Which facial lines do you evaluate?

Forehead lines, frown lines, crow's feet and lines along the sides of the nose. Some of these uses are off-label depending on the product, and we explain that during your evaluation.

Is botulinum toxin safe?

Cleveland Clinic says complications are rare, but no treatment is free of risk and all these products carry an FDA boxed warning. We review your health first and explain the risks that apply to you.

Can I have it if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?

Tell us if you are, or plan to be. It isn't known whether it can harm an unborn baby or passes into breast milk, and Cleveland Clinic lists pregnancy and breastfeeding among the reasons to avoid it.

Does facial PRF work?

The evidence is mixed. Small studies report some improvement in skin measures and patient satisfaction, and many have no control group. We can't promise rejuvenation or wrinkle removal, and we discuss these limits before you decide.

Is PRF safer because it comes from my own blood?

It's prepared from your own blood, and a 2025 review reports no serious events in the studies it included. It still has risks such as bruising and swelling, which depend on the technique, and the studies are small.
