Dentures in Miami & Hialeah

Dentures replace missing teeth with a removable set made to fit your mouth. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we make full and partial dentures for patients from all over Miami and adjust them with you until they feel right.

What are dentures?

Dentures are removable replacement teeth set in a gum-colored base. A full denture replaces all the teeth in an upper or lower arch. A partial denture fills the gaps when you still have some natural teeth, and small clasps hook onto those teeth to keep it steady.

  1. 1Artificial teeth Replace the missing teeth
  2. 2Gum-colored base Holds the teeth and rests on the gum
  3. 3Clasp Hooks onto a natural tooth
  4. 4Metal framework Connects both sides and keeps the shape

Are dentures right for you?

Dentures are an option whether you've lost a few teeth or all of them. What matters most is the health of your gums and of any teeth you still have.

Usually a good fit

  • You're missing several teeth or a full arch
  • You want a lower-cost way to replace many teeth
  • Implants aren't an option for you right now
  • You need teeth right after extractions. An immediate denture covers that

May need treatment or a closer look first

  • Gum disease or decay in the teeth that will hold a partial
  • Teeth that need to come out before the denture is made
  • An old denture that slips or hurts. It may need a reline or a new denture

If you'd rather have teeth that stay in place, we can also talk about implant-supported dentures.

Fitted and adjusted with you

We take impressions of your mouth, check the fit with you along the way and adjust the final denture at the office. When a sore spot shows up or the bite needs a tweak, we fix it at a follow-up visit.

Expect a few adjustment visits in the first couple of weeks. That's normal, and each visit makes the fit more comfortable.

How dentures are made, step by step

  1. 1

    Exam and plan

    We check your gums, your bone and any remaining teeth, and go over full, partial and implant-supported options.

  2. 2

    Extractions, if needed

    Teeth that can't be saved come out first. An immediate denture lets you wear teeth right away while your gums heal.

  3. 3

    Impressions and design

    We take impressions and measurements of your mouth and bite to design the denture.

  4. 4

    Try-in

    You try the denture on. We check the fit, the bite and the look, and make changes before it's finished.

  5. 5

    Adjustments

    It usually takes several visits to get the fit right. Sore spots are common at first, and we adjust them.

Getting used to new dentures

It can take weeks, sometimes months, to feel at home with new dentures. It gets easier with practice.

First days
Your mouth may feel full. Start with soft foods cut into small pieces and chew on both sides.
First weeks
Expect a few adjustment visits. Sore spots are normal at first, so tell us about them instead of waiting.
Speaking
Some words may sound different at first. Reading aloud helps you get used to it faster.
Every night
Take your dentures out, clean them with a denture brush and soak them overnight in denture solution.

Call us if a denture hurts, pinches your gums or keeps slipping.

Downsides of dentures

Dentures are an affordable way to replace many teeth, but they come with trade-offs you should know about.

  • Traditional dentures can shift or slip when you talk or chew.
  • The jawbone keeps shrinking after teeth are lost, so dentures loosen over time and need relines or replacement.
  • Sore spots are common at first and take a few adjustments to fix.
  • Very hard, sticky or chewy foods are harder to eat than with natural teeth.
  • A partial denture puts some load on the teeth it hooks onto, so those teeth need good care.

Regular checkups let us reline or adjust your dentures before small problems turn into sore spots.

How much do dentures cost in Miami?

The price depends on the type of denture and what your mouth needs first, so we give you a cost estimate after your exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • Full, partial or implant-supported
  • Whether teeth need to come out first
  • Whether you need an immediate denture while you heal
  • The materials of the base and the teeth
  • What your insurance covers

Denture questions

How long do dentures last?

Most dentures last 7 to 10 years. Your gums and jawbone keep changing, so a denture usually needs a reline every 1 to 2 years to keep fitting well.

What are the three types of dentures?

Full dentures replace all the teeth in an arch, partial dentures fill gaps between natural teeth, and implant-supported dentures attach to dental implants. Immediate dentures are a version you wear right after extractions.

What can you never eat again with dentures?

There's no strict list, but very hard, sticky or chewy foods, like hard candy, caramel or tough meat, are harder to manage and can loosen or damage dentures. Cutting food small and chewing on both sides helps.

Should I sleep with my dentures in?

We usually recommend taking them out at night. It gives your gums a rest, and soaking the dentures overnight keeps them clean.

How much do dentures cost in Florida?

It depends on the type, the materials and whether you need extractions first. We give you a cost estimate after your exam, check what your insurance covers and go over payment options with you.

Does Medicare cover dentures?

Original Medicare doesn't cover dentures. Some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits that can help with the cost. Bring your card and we'll check your plan with you.

What's better than dentures?

For some people, implant-supported dentures or implants feel more stable because they're attached to the jaw. They cost more and need surgery and healing time. We'll help you compare the options for your mouth and your budget.
