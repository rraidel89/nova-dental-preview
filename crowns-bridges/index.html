Dental Crowns and Bridges in Miami & Hialeah

A dental crown covers and protects a damaged tooth, and a bridge uses crowns on the teeth beside a gap to replace one that's missing. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we place dental crowns and bridges for patients from all over Miami.

What are dental crowns and bridges?

A dental crown is a tooth-shaped cap that covers a weak, cracked or worn-down tooth. A bridge replaces a missing tooth: crowns on the teeth on each side hold a replacement tooth, called a pontic, over the gap. Both are custom-made and cemented in place, so you don't take them out.

  1. 1Crown A ceramic cap that covers the shaped tooth
  2. 2Replacement tooth Fills the gap. Also called a pontic
  3. 3Shaped tooth Holds the crown and supports the bridge
  4. 4Jawbone No root under the replacement tooth

When do you need a crown or a bridge?

A crown makes sense when a tooth is still there but too weak for a filling. A bridge makes sense when a tooth is gone and the teeth beside the gap are healthy.

Usually a good fit

  • A tooth is cracked, broken or worn down
  • A large filling no longer holds a tooth together
  • A back tooth has had a root canal
  • You're missing a tooth and the teeth on each side of the gap are healthy

May need another option or treatment first

  • Decay or gum disease around the teeth that would hold the crown or bridge
  • You grind your teeth, which can crack crowns. A night guard can help
  • Three or more teeth are missing in a row. An implant-supported bridge may work better
  • A small cavity that a filling can still fix

We check the teeth around the gap before recommending a bridge, and we'll tell you if a filling or an implant is the better fit.

How a crown or bridge is placed

  1. 1

    Exam and plan

    We check the tooth, take X-rays and talk through your options and the cost.

  2. 2

    Shaping the tooth

    After numbing the area, we remove a thin layer of enamel so the crown fits over the tooth. For a bridge, we shape the teeth on both sides of the gap.

  3. 3

    Digital scan

    We scan your teeth to design the crown or bridge, and you wear a temporary one while the final restoration is made.

  4. 4

    Fitting

    We check the fit, the bite and the color, then cement the crown or bridge in place.

  5. 5

    Fine-tuning

    If your bite feels uneven after a few days, call us and we'll adjust it.

After your crown or bridge

Most people go back to work or school right after the appointment.

First day
Wait until the numbness wears off before you eat, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue.
First few days
Sore gums are normal and should settle in a few days. Over-the-counter pain relievers help.
First weeks
Some sensitivity to hot and cold is normal for a couple of weeks. Gums around a bridge usually heal in one to two weeks.
Every day after
Brush twice a day, floss once a day and clean under a bridge with floss threaders or small interdental brushes.

Call us if a crown feels loose, your bite feels off, or the pain gets worse instead of better.

Downsides of crowns and bridges

Crowns and bridges are routine, but they permanently change the teeth they cover, so it helps to know the trade-offs.

  • Some natural enamel has to be removed, and that can't be undone.
  • Teeth can be sensitive to hot and cold for the first few weeks.
  • Crowns can chip or crack over time, especially if you grind your teeth.
  • If a crown or bridge doesn't fit well, or isn't cleaned well, decay or gum inflammation can start underneath.
  • A bridge depends on the teeth that hold it. If one of them gets decay or is damaged, the bridge weakens.

Brushing, cleaning under the bridge and regular checkups protect both the restoration and the teeth that hold it.

How much do dental crowns and bridges cost in Miami?

The price depends on the tooth and the material, so we give you a cost estimate after your exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • A single crown or a bridge, and how many teeth it spans
  • The material of the crown
  • Whether the tooth needs a root canal or a buildup first
  • What your insurance covers

Crown and bridge questions

How long do dental crowns last?

With good care, most crowns last 5 to 15 years. Grinding your teeth, chewing ice and skipping cleanings shorten that. Regular checkups let us catch wear or decay at the edges early.

Do teeth go bad under crowns?

The crown itself can't decay, but the tooth under it can, usually at the edge where the crown meets the tooth. Brushing, flossing and regular cleanings are the best protection, and a crown that fits well leaves less room for bacteria.

Can I get a crown in one day?

It depends on the tooth and the type of crown. Many crowns take two visits: one to prepare the tooth and one to place the final crown. We'll tell you at your evaluation which applies to you.

Can you eat normally with a dental bridge?

Yes. Once the bridge is cemented, you chew with it like your own teeth. Avoid very hard or sticky foods such as ice and taffy, which can chip a crown or pull on it.

Is a bridge or an implant better?

An implant replaces the missing root and leaves the neighboring teeth alone, but it takes months and costs more. A bridge is faster and costs less upfront, but it relies on the teeth beside the gap. Which is better depends on your teeth and your budget.

What can be done instead of a dental crown?

If enough healthy tooth is left, a filling can sometimes do the job with less drilling. We only recommend a crown when the tooth needs the extra protection, and we'll show you why.

Does insurance cover crowns and bridges?

Many dental plans pay part of the cost, and the share varies by plan. Original Medicare doesn't cover most dental care. We check your benefits before treatment, so you know your part before you commit.
