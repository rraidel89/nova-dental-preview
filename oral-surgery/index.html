Oral Surgery and Wisdom Teeth in Miami & Hialeah

Oral surgery covers procedures that go beyond a filling, from taking out a tooth to removing a wisdom tooth stuck under the gum. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, we do extractions and wisdom teeth removal for patients from all over Miami, with sedation options when your case calls for it.

What does oral surgery include?

In a dental office, the most common oral surgery is removing a tooth. A simple extraction takes out a tooth you can see. A surgical extraction is for teeth that are broken at the gum line or impacted, like a wisdom tooth that is still under the gum and pushing against the tooth next to it.

  1. 1Second molar The tooth the wisdom tooth pushes against
  2. 2Impacted wisdom tooth Tilted and trapped under the gum
  3. 3Jawbone Surrounds the impacted tooth
  4. 4Nerve Runs close to the roots

When does a tooth need to come out?

We only take out a tooth when keeping it would cause more trouble than removing it.

Common reasons

  • A wisdom tooth that is impacted, crooked or causing pain
  • Food getting trapped around a wisdom tooth, leading to gum disease or cavities
  • A tooth damaging the teeth or bone next to it
  • A tooth too broken or infected to save
  • Making room before orthodontics or an implant

May not need surgery, or needs a closer look first

  • Wisdom teeth that are healthy, fully in and easy to clean
  • Roots that sit very close to the nerve in the jaw
  • Medicines that affect bleeding or bone healing. Tell us before any surgery

Not every wisdom tooth has to come out. We'll show you the X-ray and explain why we recommend removing a tooth or leaving it.

Comfort during surgery

Every extraction starts with local anesthesia. If you're anxious or the surgery is longer, we can offer sedation after an evaluation.

We also use laser technology in some procedures. If you have sedation, you'll need someone to drive you home, and you shouldn't drive for at least 24 hours.

How a tooth or wisdom tooth is removed

  1. 1

    Exam and X-ray

    We check the position of the tooth, its roots and how close they are to the nerve.

  2. 2

    Anesthesia

    We numb the area. If you've chosen sedation, we give it before we start.

  3. 3

    Removal

    A simple extraction loosens and lifts the tooth out. An impacted tooth may need a small opening in the gum and may come out in pieces.

  4. 4

    Closing

    We place gauze to stop the bleeding and, when needed, a few stitches.

  5. 5

    Follow-up

    We check the healing and, if you're replacing the tooth, plan the next step.

Wisdom teeth recovery, day by day

Full recovery takes about two weeks, and the socket keeps healing for more than a month.

Days 1 to 3
Light bleeding, swelling and pain are expected. Use ice for 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off, and eat soft foods.
Days 4 to 7
Most people go back to their routine by day 5. Start rinsing gently with warm salt water.
Week 2
Side effects are usually gone, but the gums still need care. Keep away from hard, crunchy foods.
Weeks 3 and 4
The gum starts to grow over and close the socket.

Don't drink through a straw, and skip fizzy drinks and alcohol for at least five days. Call us if swelling gets worse after day 3, you see pus, you have a fever or the bleeding won't stop.

Risks of oral surgery

Wisdom teeth removal usually doesn't cause long-term problems, but you should know what can happen.

  • Dry socket: the blood clot comes loose and leaves bone exposed. It's rare and very painful, and it usually shows up two to four days after surgery.
  • Infection, with pus coming from the socket or the stitches.
  • Damage to nearby teeth, the jawbone, the sinus or the nerves.
  • Swelling and bruising for the first few days.

Following the aftercare steps, especially not using straws, protects the blood clot while the socket heals.

How much does wisdom teeth removal cost in Miami?

The price depends on how many teeth come out and how difficult they are, so we give you a cost estimate after the exam. These are the things that move it up or down:

  • How many teeth are removed
  • Simple or surgical extraction, and whether the tooth is impacted
  • Sedation, if you choose it
  • A bone graft, if an implant is planned
  • What your insurance covers

We work with many dental plans, including Delta Dental, MetLife, Cigna, Aetna and Humana, and we check your coverage before you start. Many plans pay part of an extraction. Oral surgery questions

Do all wisdom teeth need to be removed?

No. Wisdom teeth that are healthy, fully in and easy to clean can often stay, with regular checkups. We recommend removal when a tooth is impacted, causes pain or puts the teeth around it at risk.

Is 27 too old to get wisdom teeth removed?

No. If you're old enough to have wisdom teeth, you're old enough to have them removed. Many dentists suggest the late teens or early 20s because the roots are shorter, but adults have them out every day.

How many days of rest do I need after wisdom teeth removal?

Most people go back to their routine by day 5. Plan on taking it easy for the first two or three days, and expect full recovery in about two weeks.

Will my face change after wisdom teeth removal?

Your cheeks may swell for the first few days, and the swelling usually eases within a week. Removing wisdom teeth doesn't change the shape of your face.

What is a dry socket?

It happens when the blood clot in the socket comes loose and leaves the bone exposed. It's rare but painful, and the pain usually starts two to four days after surgery. Call us if the pain gets worse instead of better.

Can I drive after a tooth extraction?

With local anesthesia only, usually yes. If you have sedation, you need someone to drive you home, and you shouldn't drive for at least 24 hours.

Does tooth extraction hurt?

The area is numbed, so you should feel pressure but not pain during the extraction. Soreness and swelling afterward are normal for a few days, and we'll tell you which pain relievers to take.
