Gum Care and Periodontal Treatment in Miami & Hialeah

Do your gums bleed, feel tender or look like they've pulled back? Healthy gums support a healthy smile, and untreated periodontal disease can damage the supporting tissues and bone and may lead to tooth mobility or loss. At Nova Dental Studio in Hialeah, periodontal care may include scaling and root planing, periodontal maintenance, Perio Protect and selected soft-tissue laser procedures, depending on clinical needs. We explain the findings, treatment options and recommended maintenance.

What is gum disease?

Gum disease is an infection that inflames the tissues holding your teeth in place. It usually starts with plaque, a sticky film of bacteria that can harden into tartar, and only a professional cleaning removes tartar. The early form, gingivitis, makes gums red, swollen and quick to bleed, and it can be reversed. In periodontitis, the gap between gum and tooth deepens into a pocket, and the tissue and bone around the tooth start to break down. Periodontitis can't be reversed, but it can be slowed and managed.

  1. 1Healthy gum Fits snugly around the tooth
  2. 2Tartar on the root Hardened plaque that brushing can't remove
  3. 3Swollen gum The gum pulls away from the tooth and forms a deep pocket
  4. 4Bone loss The bone holding the tooth breaks down

Gum disease symptoms: when to get checked

The CDC estimates that about 4 in 10 U.S. adults aged 30 or older had some level of periodontitis in 2009 to 2014. It often becomes serious before you notice much, and it may not hurt, so regular checkups matter.

Signs to get checked

  • Red, swollen or tender gums
  • Gums that bleed when you brush or floss
  • Gums that have pulled back, so your teeth look longer
  • Bad breath that doesn't go away
  • Loose or sensitive teeth, or pain when you chew
  • A change in how your teeth meet, or a partial denture that no longer fits well

What raises your risk

  • Smoking or using tobacco, which also delays healing
  • Diabetes
  • Older age and family history
  • Hormonal changes, such as during pregnancy or menopause
  • Stress, or medications with side effects in the mouth
  • Grinding or clenching your teeth

Only an exam can tell what's going on. Early gum disease can often be reversed with daily brushing and flossing, while deeper disease may need a deep cleaning, and advanced cases may need surgery. If your case needs more than we've described here, including gum grafting when clinically indicated, we'll explain your options.

How we care for your gums at Nova

We check your gums with a small probe that measures the pockets around your teeth, and we take X-rays when indicated to look for bone loss. Healthy pockets are usually 1 to 3 millimeters, and deeper ones can point to periodontal disease.

For a deep cleaning, we numb the area and use hand instruments and a Cavitron ultrasonic scaler to remove deposits above and below the gumline. If nerves make the visit hard, ask us about nitrous oxide.

Care based on your diagnosis

A routine cleaning, deep cleaning and periodontal maintenance address different needs, so we start with your exam.

A nonsurgical first step

Scaling and root planing is usually the first treatment for mild to moderate gum disease, with no incisions or stitches.

Follow-up set for you

After treatment, periodontal maintenance helps lower the chance of infection returning. We set the interval with your history in mind.

Cleanings and gum care

Caring for your smile starts with understanding the health of your teeth and gums. We provide examinations, cleanings and home-care guidance. If periodontal disease is identified, we explain the findings, treatment options and recommended maintenance. A routine cleaning, deep cleaning and periodontal maintenance address different needs; care is selected according to the diagnosis.

  • Routine cleaning: preventive care for a healthy mouth
  • Scaling and root planing, or deep cleaning: treats deposits and inflammation below the gumline when clinically indicated
  • Periodontal maintenance: ongoing care after periodontal treatment. It doesn't serve as a first treatment

Perio Protect

Perio Protect uses custom trays to deliver prescribed medication near the gumline at home as an adjunct to professional treatment; it does not replace deep cleaning or periodontal maintenance.

We consider it only in indicated cases. If it fits yours, we explain how and when to wear the trays.

Soft-tissue laser

Nova Dental Studio offers soft-tissue laser care for gum procedures, gingivectomy and frenectomy, and as an adjunct to periodontal treatment. We assess your needs and explain when laser care may be part of your treatment plan.

Soft-tissue lasers may provide precise treatment and help control bleeding in selected procedures. Some patients experience less postoperative discomfort, but outcomes vary and laser treatment is not required for every patient.

Published reviews comparing laser and scalpel for these procedures found less bleeding and fewer stitches with the laser. The studies are small and varied, and they don't show that any one laser is clearly better.

  • Gingivectomy removes or reshapes gum tissue
  • Frenectomy removes the fold of tissue that attaches the lip to the gum, when it causes problems
  • As an adjunct to periodontal treatment, in selected cases

How a deep cleaning is done, step by step

  1. 1

    Exam and measurements

    We review your health history, measure the pockets around your teeth and take X-rays when indicated. Then we explain the findings and your options.

  2. 2

    Numbing

    We numb your gums with local anesthesia. You stay awake, and the treatment may take one visit or be split into two.

  3. 3

    Scaling

    We remove plaque and tartar above and below the gumline, using hand and ultrasonic instruments.

  4. 4

    Root planing

    We smooth the tooth roots, which helps keep plaque and tartar from reattaching. If medication is indicated, we explain it to you.

  5. 5

    Re-evaluation

    We check your gums again, often about a month later, and measure the pockets. That tells us whether you can move to periodontal maintenance or need further treatment.

Recovery after a deep cleaning

Most people go back to their routine the same day. Recovery from other gum procedures varies, and we explain what to expect for yours.

Day of treatment
Wait until the numbness wears off before eating, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue. Then eat and drink whatever doesn't hurt.
First couple of days
Your gums may be sore, and some bleeding is normal. Over-the-counter pain relievers can help.
Next 3 to 4 weeks
Bleeding and tenderness typically resolve in this time. Keep brushing and flossing every day, and ask us to show you the technique.
A month or two
Sensitivity to heat and cold should go away. Teeth that feel a little loose should settle as your gums tighten.
After that
Keep up your home care and your periodontal maintenance visits, since gum disease can come back.

Call us at 786-687-0909 if the bleeding doesn't stop, the pain doesn't improve with medication or you have severe swelling. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, or swelling that spreads quickly, seek emergency care immediately.

Deep cleaning risks and side effects

Cleveland Clinic describes complications as rare but possible. Most of what people notice is temporary.

  • Tenderness, which usually lasts a couple of days
  • Bleeding, which is normal to some degree
  • Tooth sensitivity, which usually resolves within a month or two
  • Gum recession, as swollen gums shrink once the infection clears
  • Loose teeth, which usually settle
  • Infection, which is uncommon. Antibiotics may reduce the risk in some cases

Treatment doesn't end the need for care at home, and gum disease can return. Tobacco delays healing and can make treatment less successful. If the disease comes back, we'll talk through further treatment.

Gum disease and deep cleaning questions

Why do my gums bleed?

Bleeding may indicate inflammation or other conditions. A dental evaluation helps identify the cause.

Regular vs deep cleaning?

A regular cleaning is preventive care. Scaling and root planing treats deposits and inflammation below the gumline when clinically indicated.

Can gum disease be reversed?

Gingivitis, the early form, can be reversed. Periodontitis can't be reversed, but treatment and daily care can slow and manage it.

Can gum disease cause tooth loss?

Untreated periodontal disease can damage the supporting tissues and bone and may lead to tooth mobility or loss.

How long does a deep cleaning take?

Usually one to two hours. It may be done in one visit or split into two, with half of your teeth treated each time, depending on your situation and your dental plan.

Does a deep cleaning hurt?

We numb your gums first with local anesthesia. Afterward, your gums may be sore for a couple of days and your teeth may be sensitive to heat and cold for a month or two.

How long is recovery?

Recovery depends on the procedure and oral health. Your dentist will explain expected healing and aftercare.

How often is periodontal maintenance needed?

Intervals are personalized according to disease history, current findings and risk factors.

Does Perio Protect replace deep cleaning?

No. It is a prescribed at-home adjunct, not a substitute for necessary professional treatment.

Does the laser replace periodontal treatment?

No. It may be used in selected cases as an adjunct or for soft-tissue procedures.

Is laser treatment painful?

Comfort depends on the procedure and patient. Anesthesia may be used; temporary sensitivity is possible.

What is VELscope, and can it detect oral cancer?

VELscope is a light-based tool that supports the evaluation of the tissues in your mouth. It doesn't replace a diagnosis and can't guarantee it will detect or rule out cancer. The American Dental Association advises against using light-based devices to decide whether a biopsy is needed, and a biopsy remains the standard for diagnosis.
